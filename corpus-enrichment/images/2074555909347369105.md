- THE SUM OF n CONSECUTIVE INTEGERS

- THE PROBLEM: What is the sum of the first n consecutive integers? · From 1 to n, the sequence: 1, 2, 3, ..., n−1, n.
- THE GAUSSIAN PROOF — CLASSIC PAIRING METHOD
- Let S = 1 + 2 + 3 + ... + (n−1) + n. . . (1)
- Now write the sum in reverse order:
- S = n + (n−1) + (n−2) + ... + 2 + 1. . . (2)
- Add the two equations term by term:
- 2S = (1+n) + (2+(n−1)) + (3+(n−2)) + ... + (n+1)
- Each pair sums to (n + 1). There are n such pairs.
- Thus, 2S = n(n+1).
- S = n(n+1)/2
- Example: For n = 5, the sum is 1+2+3+4+5 = 5×6/2 = 15 ✓
- WHY IT WORKS: This pairing method reveals the symmetry of the series—no induction needed. As elegant as it is efficient, the proof is attributed to Gauss when he was just 9 years old.
