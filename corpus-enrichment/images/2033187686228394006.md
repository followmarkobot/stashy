The meta-opinion most devs would disagree with

gstack's deepest assumption is: workflows should be encoded in prompts, not in code. The ship process, review checklist, QA methodology, retro format — all live in Markdown files that instruct an LLM.

Most developers would say: "That's not software engineering. That's prompt engineering. It's untestable, undebuggable, and non-deterministic. When the LLM misinterprets a sentence in your SKILL.md, you don't get a stack trace — you get a subtly wrong PR."

gstack's counter-argument (which I think is genuinely novel) is that the eval infrastructure — tiered testing from free static validation to $4 LLM-judge runs — is the substitute for traditional testing. But that's a bet on a paradigm most developers haven't accepted yet.
